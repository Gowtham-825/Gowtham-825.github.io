PROJECT CASE STUDY · 01

Smart Two-Wheeler Energy Optimization Using IoT

A hardware-based energy management module integrating battery monitoring, renewable-energy integration, active thermal management and emergency wireless charging for an electric two-wheeler application.

STM32 Microcontroller Energy Management Battery Monitoring Solar Dynamo Thermal Management Wireless Charging

01 Project Overview

This project presents a multi-source hardware energy management module designed for electric two-wheelers. The system integrates battery monitoring, renewable-energy integration, active thermal management and emergency wireless charging within a single hardware platform.

A 12 V lithium-ion battery is monitored using voltage, current and temperature sensing. Solar and dynamo sources are incorporated as supplementary charging sources, while the STM32-based control unit manages sensing, protection and energy-management functions.

02 Goal

  • Continuously monitor battery voltage, current and temperature.
  • Protect the battery from over-voltage, under-voltage and overheating.
  • Integrate solar and dynamo sources as supplementary charging sources.
  • Select and manage the available charging source.
  • Automatically operate the cooling system when temperature rises.
  • Provide emergency short-range wireless energy transfer.
  • Validate sensing and energy-management functions experimentally.

03 System Architecture

The architecture combines energy sources, battery storage, sensing, STM32-based control and protection/output stages.

04 Build Process

STEP 01

Battery & Sensing

A 12 V lithium-ion battery was used as the energy-storage unit. Voltage, current and temperature sensing were incorporated for continuous monitoring.

STEP 02

STM32 Control

The STM32 microcontroller was used as the central embedded control unit for sensor acquisition, processing and control decisions.

STEP 03

Renewable Integration

Solar and dynamo sources were integrated as supplementary charging sources with source selection and switching logic.

STEP 04

Thermal & Emergency Functions

A temperature-controlled cooling fan and an emergency short-range wireless power-transfer module were integrated and tested.

05 Fault Detection and EFD Logic

The system continuously monitors battery voltage, current and temperature and compares measured values with predefined safe operating limits. The STM32-based control logic uses these conditions to initiate the required protection or corrective response.

Protection / Response
  • Under-voltage: non-essential loads are switched out to avoid complete discharge.
  • Over-voltage: charging is limited when the battery reaches its maximum safe voltage.
  • Over-temperature: the cooling system is activated when battery temperature rises above the defined threshold.

06 Challenges and Engineering Decisions

  • Integrating multiple energy sources into one hardware energy-management architecture.
  • Reliable acquisition and validation of voltage, current and temperature measurements.
  • Maintaining battery safety limits during charging and operation.
  • Implementing solar/dynamo source selection and switching.
  • Providing automatic thermal protection using a cooling fan.
  • Integrating emergency wireless power transfer into the prototype.
  • Keeping the system modular, practical and cost-conscious.

07 Results and Evidence

The prototype was experimentally validated using reference instruments and different operating conditions. The measured sensor values showed close agreement with reference readings.

Voltage Sensing 99.68%, 99.59%, 99.41%, 99.30% and 99.37% accuracy across the tested battery conditions.
Temperature Sensing 99.33% to 99.75% accuracy across the 30°C–50°C reference range.
Solar Output 12.5 W recorded during daytime; 0 W during nighttime testing.
Dynamo Output 6.2 W during daytime testing and 6.0 W during nighttime testing.
Cooling Response Temperature reduced from approximately 50°C to 40°C in about 4 minutes after fan activation.
Wireless Charging Recipient battery voltage increased by approximately 0.3 V during the 15-minute test.
